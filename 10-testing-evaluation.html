Testing & Evaluating

A design isn't finished when you finish making it — it's finished when you've checked it actually does what it was meant to do, and reflected honestly on how it went.

Learning intention

You can test your design against your own success criteria, and evaluate your design process and outcome.

Explicit teaching

Testing against success criteria

Go back to the success criteria you wrote in Phase 3. For each one, test your finished (or nearly finished) CyberPet and record a clear pass/partial/fail — with evidence, not just a guess.

  • Test the same way for every criterion so results are fair
  • Where possible, get someone else (a peer or your teacher) to test it too
  • Record exactly what happened, not what you hoped would happen

Evaluating

Evaluating means judging how well your design and your process worked, and what you'd change.

  • What worked well, and why?
  • What didn't meet a criterion, and why not?
  • If you had more time, what specifically would you change?
  • What did you learn about the design process itself?

Linked resources

  • Reminder Phase 3: Generating Success Criteria — open this in another tab to check your original criteria while you test.

Activity

Test your CyberPet (or your final design, if it wasn't physically made) against every success criterion from Phase 3. Record the result and evidence for each, then write an honest evaluation of your design and your process.

Your response

  • I have tested against every success criterion from Phase 3
  • Each test result has real evidence, not just an opinion
  • I have written an honest evaluation, including what didn't work
  • I have reflected on my design process, not just the final product
