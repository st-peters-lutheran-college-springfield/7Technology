Generating Success Criteria

Turn the design brief into testable statements you can actually check your work against later.

Learning intention

You can write clear, testable design criteria for your CyberPet, including sustainability.

Explicit teaching

What is a design criterion?

A design criterion is a testable statement describing what your finished product must do or be. Good criteria are specific enough that someone could check "yes" or "no" against them once the product is finished.

Weak: "It should look nice."
Strong: "The case fits within a 15 × 15 cm footprint and fully encloses the micro:bit, GlowBit and battery pack."

Sustainability — mandatory

Every folio must address all three dimensions:

  • Social — supports wellbeing, inclusive, non-judgemental
  • Environmental — responsible materials, reduced waste, durability
  • Economic — cost-effective, repairable, scalable

Linked resources

  • Read Design Fundamentals guide (UC Berkeley Library) — background on how designers set and use criteria.

Activity

Write at least six design criteria — start from the non-negotiable constraints, then add your own persona-based goals (colour, texture, feel).

Then write one short paragraph for each sustainability dimension, linking it to an actual decision you've made — not a generic claim.

Your response

  • I have written at least six specific, testable criteria
  • My criteria include all the non-negotiable constraints
  • I've written a statement for social, environmental AND economic sustainability
  • Each sustainability statement links to a specific decision, not a generic claim
