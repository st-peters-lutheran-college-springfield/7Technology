Sketching & Annotating Ideas

One idea is never enough. Generate several different concepts for your CyberPet case, then annotate them clearly enough that someone else could understand your thinking without you there to explain it.

Learning intention

You can generate multiple design concepts and annotate a sketch so it communicates your design thinking to someone else.

Explicit teaching

Generating multiple concepts

Designers sketch several different ideas before choosing one — the first idea is rarely the best idea. Vary shape, orientation, how the LEDs show through, and how it opens for the electronics.

  • Aim for at least three genuinely different concepts, not three small variations
  • Quick and rough is fine at this stage — these are thinking sketches, not final art
  • Keep referring back to your persona (Phase 2) and success criteria (Phase 3)

What makes a sketch "annotated"

An annotated sketch has labels and notes added around the drawing that explain the parts a picture alone can't show.

  • Materials — what each part is made from
  • Dimensions — approximate sizes, using your Phase 7 model as a guide
  • Function callouts — arrows/labels explaining what a feature does (e.g. "LED window — shows mood colour")
  • Design links — a short note on which success criterion or persona need each idea addresses

Linked resources

  • Guide How to Sketch Your Design Ideas (Core77) — practical sketching approach for beginners.
  • Example Isometric Drawing Guide (Autodesk) — reuse your Phase 7 isometric skills for cleaner concept sketches.
