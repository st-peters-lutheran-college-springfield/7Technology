Exploring Materials

Every material has different properties and purposes. Choose ones suited to your persona and the CyberPet's function.

Learning intention

You understand that materials have different properties and purposes, and you can select materials suited to your chosen persona and the CyberPet's function.

Explicit teaching

Your CyberPet must be made from one or more approved materials only: cardboard, felt/leather (synthetic or recycled preferred), plywood, acrylic (teacher-approved), or 3D-printed components.

Cardboard

✔ Recyclable, easy to prototype, low cost
✕ Low durability, weakens with handling

Felt / leather

✔ Soft, calming, adds comfort
✕ Needs structural support underneath

Plywood

✔ Durable, laser-cuttable, precise
✕ Higher resource use, needs tool access

Acrylic & 3D-printed parts

✔ Precise, repeatable, can be transparent/coloured
✕ Acrylic needs approval; 3D printing is slower and uses plastic filament

Linked resources

  • Datasheets Trotec material safety & technical datasheets — see how plywood and acrylic are actually rated for laser cutting.

Activity

Rate at least two materials out of 5 against three questions: does it suit your persona's feel, is it durable enough for daily desk use and being carried between classrooms, and is it a responsible material choice?

Then explain the difference between additive manufacturing (e.g. 3D printing — building up material) and subtractive manufacturing (e.g. laser cutting — removing material from a larger piece). You'll need to understand both (see Phase 6).

Your response

  • I have rated at least two materials against my persona
  • I can explain additive vs. subtractive manufacturing
  • I understand plain cardboard alone probably won't meet the durability requirement
