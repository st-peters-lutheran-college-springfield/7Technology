Manufacturing Techniques

Additive manufacturing (3D printing) and subtractive manufacturing (Trotec laser cutting) — how each works, and how to use them safely.

Learning intention

You can explain two manufacturing approaches and how they apply safely to your design.

Explicit teaching

Additive manufacturing — 3D printing

Builds an object up, layer by layer, from a digital model (your TinkerCad file — see Phase 7).

  • Good for precise, complex, small parts — like an internal mount or a decorative feature
  • Slower to produce than cutting a flat sheet
  • Uses plastic filament, layer by layer

Subtractive manufacturing — laser cutting

Removes material from a larger sheet (like plywood or acrylic) using a laser, guided by a digital cutting file.

  • Good for flat panels, cases and structural pieces
  • Fast and very precise for 2D shapes
  • Our laser cutters are Trotec machines — see the safety rules below

Laser cutter safety — non-negotiable

  • Never leave a running laser cutter unattended — it must be watched the entire time it is cutting
  • Always wear safety glasses and keep the machine cover closed while it's operating
  • Only teacher-approved materials go near the laser — some materials (like PVC) release dangerous fumes and must never be used
  • Confirm the fume extraction system is running before cutting or engraving
  • Only use the laser cutter after a teacher safety demonstration — never independently the first time

Linked resources

  • Safety Trotec EDU Laser Safety — safety guidance written specifically for school laser use.
  • FAQ How safe is a laser engraver? — Trotec's official safety FAQ.
  • Datasheets Trotec material safety & technical datasheets — check any material before it goes near the laser.

Activity

For your chosen design (from Phase 8), decide which parts would use additive manufacturing, which would use subtractive manufacturing, and why. You don't have to physically make your product using both methods — you can explain how each could be used appropriately.

List at least three safe work practices that apply to the tools and materials you plan to use.
