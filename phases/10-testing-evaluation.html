Testing & Evaluating Against Success Criteria

Test your design honestly, document improvements, then step back and evaluate the whole project.

Learning intention

You can test your design against your criteria, document specific improvements, and justify your key decisions.

Explicit teaching

Testing against your criteria

Go back to the design criteria you wrote in Phase 3. For each one, record pass / partial / fail with real evidence — a measurement, a photo, a description of what happened when you tested it. Specifically check: fit, stability, durability, and portability.

Example: Criterion — "The case fits within a 15 × 15 cm footprint." Result — Pass, measured footprint 14 × 13.5 cm.

Evaluating — the final step

Choose your three most important design decisions and justify each: what you decided, what alternatives you considered, and why — referencing your persona, materials investigation and testing evidence, not just personal preference.

Then check your finished design against the assessment's success criteria one last time before submission.

Linked resources

  • Read Design Fundamentals guide (UC Berkeley Library) — a refresher on evaluating design decisions against criteria.

Activity

Test your design against every criterion from Phase 3 and record the evidence. For anything that didn't fully pass, note the problem, what you changed, and why that fixes it.

Then write your final reflection (150–200 words): the hardest part of this challenge, what you'd do differently, and one thing you learned about human-centred design.
