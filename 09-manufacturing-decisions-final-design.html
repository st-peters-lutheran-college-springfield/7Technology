Phase 9 of 10 · Feeds Folio Sections 6 & 8

Final Design & Manufacturing Plan

Select your one final idea, document exactly how it will be made, and describe it clearly enough for anyone to understand.

Learning intention

You can justify a final design choice, plan its manufacture safely, and describe the finished design clearly using technical language.

Explicit teaching

What your manufacturing plan needs

1
Materials

Which approved material(s), and why (link back to Phase 4).

2
Method(s)

Additive, subtractive, hand tools, or a mix (link back to Phase 6).

3
Assembly

How pieces fit together and how electronics mount securely.

4
Safety

Which safe practices apply to your tools and materials.

Fitting the electronics

Your case has to securely house a real micro:bit, GlowBit and battery pack — not just look good on paper. Check real dimensions and mounting options before finalising your case size.

Linked resources

  • Guide The micro:bit User Guide for Absolute Beginners (Kitronik) — hardware basics and dimensions.
  • Product ZIP LEDs Add-On Pack (Kitronik) — the type of addressable LED strip your GlowBit uses.

Activity

Look back at your sketches (Phase 8) and choose the one idea you will take forward. Justify why, referencing your persona, criteria and the idea checklist.

Then write your manufacturing plan, and a final design description someone unfamiliar with the project could understand — including how your design meets every non-negotiable constraint.

Your response

  • I have selected and justified one final design idea
  • I have documented materials, methods, assembly and safety
  • I have checked my design against every non-negotiable constraint
  • I have used correct technical terms throughout

Absent from class?

If you missed a workshop/tool demonstration lesson, do not use any tool you haven't been shown how to use safely. Write your plan from this page, then arrange a catch-up safety briefing with your teacher before any hands-on making.
