The Design Challenge

Before you design anything, understand the real problem you're solving — who it's for, and why it matters.

Learning intention

You can explain, in your own words, the real-world problem this design challenge is asking you to solve.

Explicit teaching

Designing for wellbeing

Starting secondary school is a big shift. Many Year 7 students feel shy, anxious or unsure in their first weeks and months — and it isn't always easy for a teacher to notice this in a room of 25–30 students. Teachers want a simple, non-verbal way to understand how their class is feeling so they can respond and support student wellbeing sooner.

Your task is to design a CyberPet: a small desktop device that sits on a teacher's desk and uses light to communicate the general mood of the class — without singling anyone out.

Uses a micro:bit + GlowBit Communicates mood with light Wireless & anonymous Housed in a case you design

Why this is a genuine design problem

  • Real users — a student choosing to share a mood, and a teacher trying to read the room
  • Real constraint — the system must never identify who sent what, or store personal data
  • Real purpose — supporting classroom wellbeing without adding pressure or surveillance

Non-negotiable constraints

  • Must be one complete unit (micro:bit + GlowBit + battery pack)
  • Must sit securely on a desk, be sturdy, portable, and protect the electronics
  • Max footprint 15 × 15 cm, max height 20 cm, must fit in a school bag
  • Must NOT identify individuals, store personal data, or track mood over time

Linked resources

  • Read What is Human-Centred Design? — the design approach this whole unit follows: starting with real people's needs, not just what looks good.

Activity

Write a problem statement in your own words. A strong problem statement answers three questions:

1
Who?

Who is this for — the student sending the signal, and the teacher receiving it?

2
What?

What difficulty do they currently face?

3
Why?

Why does solving it matter?

Your response

"[User] needs a way to [do something] because [reason], but currently [what makes this hard]."

  • I can explain what the CyberPet system does, in one or two sentences
  • I understand the system must stay anonymous — no names, no stored data, no individual tracking
  • I have written my own problem statement above
  • I understand this is a physical product, not just code
