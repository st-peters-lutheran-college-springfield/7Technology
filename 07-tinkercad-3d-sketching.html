TinkerCad & 3D Sketching

Before you commit to a final case design, you need two ways to show your ideas in 3D: a digital model in TinkerCad, and quick isometric sketches on paper.

Learning intention

You can build a simple 3D model in TinkerCad and draw a basic isometric sketch of an object.

Explicit teaching

What is TinkerCad?

TinkerCad is a free, browser-based 3D design tool. You build models by combining simple solid shapes — boxes, cylinders, holes — the same way you'll need to combine shapes to design a CyberPet case.

  • Log in with your school account — no download needed
  • Shapes snap to a grid, so measurements stay accurate
  • A "hole" shape cuts material away — useful for cable slots or LED windows

Isometric sketching basics

An isometric sketch shows an object in 3D using three axes at 120° to each other, so you can see the top, front and side of an object in one drawing.

  • Vertical lines stay vertical
  • The other two sets of lines go at 30° above the horizontal
  • Isometric grid/dot paper makes this much easier than freehand

Linked resources

  • Tutorial TinkerCad Lessons — official beginner lessons; complete "Tinkercad 3D Design Overview" if you're new to it.
  • Guide Isometric Drawing: A Beginner's Guide — how to draw in isometric by hand.

Activity

In TinkerCad: build a simple block model roughly the size of your CyberPet case (within the Phase 1 constraints), and cut one opening for the GlowBit LEDs. Note your model's approximate dimensions below — you don't need a finished design yet.

On paper: draw one isometric sketch of the same block model, labelling its width, depth and height.

Your response

  • I have built a simple block model in TinkerCad within my size constraints
  • I have cut at least one opening/hole in my model
  • I have drawn one isometric sketch with labelled dimensions
  • I understand vertical lines stay vertical and the other lines sit at 30°

Absent from class?

TinkerCad works from any browser at home — log in with your school account and complete the activity there. If you don't have isometric paper, sketch on plain paper using a ruler and a 30° set square, or print isometric paper from the guide linked above.
